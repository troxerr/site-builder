Signs it's a repair, not a replacement

A single ceiling stain, shingles missing from one slope, or water that only appears during a hard rain usually points to a localized failure. The usual suspects:

  • A cracked or missing pipe boot
  • Failed step flashing at a wall or chimney
  • Wind-lifted ridge or field shingles
  • A clogged or separated valley
  • Nail pops or a small area of damaged decking

What the inspection covers

We walk the roof when it's safe, check the attic where accessible, and photograph what we find. We look at the flashing details, the valleys, the penetrations, and the ventilation — not just the spot where the stain shows up.

You get the photos, an explanation of the likely path of the water, and a written scope. If a repair will solve it, we say so. If the damage is widespread, we explain why a replacement may cost less over the next few years.

What a repair typically costs

Repairs vary with access, materials, and how much decking is affected. We don't publish a flat price because a $400 boot replacement and a $4,000 valley rebuild are not the same job. The inspection is free; the scope is written before any work begins.
