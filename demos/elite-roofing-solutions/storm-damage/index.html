Storm damage and insurance claims.

Hail, wind, and fallen limbs damage roofs in ways that are hard to see from the ground. Here is the order of operations — and who is responsible for each step.

Request a roof inspection (901) 435-6250

The sequence

What happens, and in what order.

Coverage is decided by your carrier. Our job is the roof and the documentation.

  1. 01

    Make the roof safe and call us

    If water is coming in, move belongings and use a bucket. Do not climb the roof. Call or request an inspection; we will tell you what can wait until morning and what cannot.

    You
  2. 02

    Inspect and document

    We photograph the damage, measure the affected area, and mark test squares for hail or wind. We check flashing, vents, and the attic where accessible. This documentation is yours to use with your insurer.

    We
  3. 03

    Open the claim

    Call your insurance company and start a claim. You are the policyholder; the claim and the coverage decision are yours. Give them our documentation and share the claim number with us.

    You
  4. 04

    Meet the adjuster

    When scheduling allows, we walk the roof with your adjuster and provide measurements, photos, and code-related items that are easy to miss. We do not negotiate your policy for you.

    We
  5. 05

    Coverage is decided

    Your carrier approves, adjusts, or denies the claim. We do not control that outcome and we do not promise approval. Once you have an approved scope, we schedule the work and confirm materials in writing.

    Your insurer

Photograph these before anything is cleaned up

  • Wide shots of each slope, plus close-ups of missing or creased shingles
  • Dents on vents, gutter caps, and the AC condenser fins
  • Water stains on ceilings, rafters, and insulation
  • Any interior damage and the belongings affected
  • Receipts for tarps, fans, dehumidifiers, or a hotel stay

What not to do

  • Don't sign a direction-to-pay with someone who knocked on your door after the storm.
  • Don't throw away damaged material before the adjuster has seen it.
  • Don't accept a scope you don't understand — ask for the line items.
  • Don't let anyone promise to "waive" your deductible. It is your responsibility.

What we document

We photograph the roof and attic, measure the affected area, note the pitch and access, and record the material and code items that affect the scope. You get a copy. If your adjuster needs to meet on site, we make the time when scheduling allows.

No claim guarantees. We are a roofing contractor, not a public adjuster or an insurer. We do not approve claims, estimate coverage, or promise a payout. We document the roof and install the approved scope.
