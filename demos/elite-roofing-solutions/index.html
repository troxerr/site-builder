A common Mid-South repair

Wind lifts a ridge section, water tracks along the decking, and the stain shows up on a ceiling two rooms away. The fix is usually flashing and a section of shingle — not a whole roof. We document the path before recommending anything.

Storm damage

After the storm, in order.

Five steps, who does what, and where your insurer enters the process. No promises about approval.

  1. 01

    Report the damage

    Call or request an inspection. Tell us where the water is showing up and when it started. If water is active, move belongings away from the leak.

    You
  2. 02

    Inspect and document

    We check the roof, flashing, and attic where accessible. We photograph the damage, measure the affected area, and mark test squares for hail or wind.

    We
  3. 03

    File the claim

    Call your insurer and open a claim. You are the policyholder — the claim is yours. Share the claim number and adjuster contact with us.

    You
  4. 04

    Meet the adjuster

    We walk the roof with the adjuster when scheduling allows and provide the measurements and photos they need to evaluate the damage.

    We
  5. 05

    Coverage is decided

    Your carrier approves, adjusts, or denies the claim. We do not control that outcome. Once you have an approved scope, we schedule the work.

    Your insurer

Questions

The things people ask first.

If your question isn't here, call (901) 435-6250. A person answers during office hours.

How do I know whether to repair or replace?+

It depends on the age of the roof, how much of it is affected, and whether water is already reaching the decking or attic. A localized problem — a failed pipe boot, one valley, a wind-lifted section — is usually a repair. Widespread granule loss, multiple leaks, or a roof near the end of its service life is usually a replacement conversation.

We inspect first and give you both numbers when both are realistic. We do not recommend a replacement when a repair will solve the problem.

How does the online roof price work?+

It starts with your property address, then asks for roof size, material, and pitch. You get a ballpark range before anyone calls you. The online tool is a planning estimate — the final number comes from an on-site inspection that accounts for decking, access, flashing, and ventilation.

What happens after I request an inspection?+

A member of our team calls you from (901) 435-6250 during office hours to confirm the address and what you are seeing. We schedule a visit, inspect the roof and attic where accessible, and follow up with photos and a written scope. There is no obligation to move forward.

Do you help with insurance claims?+

We document storm damage with photos, measurements, and test squares, and we meet your adjuster on site when scheduling allows. Your carrier decides coverage — we do not approve or guarantee claims. We can help you understand the scope and the code items that are often missed.

How long does a roof replacement take?+

Most single-family asphalt shingle replacements take one to three days of production once materials are on site. Larger roofs, steep pitches, and decking repairs add time. You get a written schedule before work starts.

Do you work on commercial and multi-family buildings?+

Yes. We install and repair low-slope systems including TPO and modified bitumen, and we work with property managers on multi-family buildings. Commercial work is scheduled around tenants and business hours.
